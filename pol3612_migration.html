International Migration

Aristide Zolberg argued that international migration is a political phenomenon: every crossing brings sovereign states into contact with each other and with individuals. This course looks at those politics (the theories, the institutions and the current debates) through three lenses: migrants, refugees and diasporas.

You don't have to read this site in order. Use the Weekly Syllabus to see what's due, and the other tabs as a toolkit you can come back to at any point in the term.

Key Terms

The vocabulary of migration is contested: the choice between "irregular" and "illegal", or between "refugee" and "migrant", is itself political. Definitions here are written in plain language and follow IOM and UNHCR usage where one exists. Filter by lens or search.

One country, many roles

Türkiye in Focus

Türkiye is often described as a country that moved "from emigration to immigration" (Kirişci 2007). It is more accurate to say it plays all of the roles below at the same time. Each card sets out one role, the policies and institutions attached to it, and a question for debate.

The BAU Migration Story Podcast: step by step

The podcast is built up over the term. Each step is a piece of individual work that feeds into your group's final episode, so choose your theme early and keep to each deadline.

    Interview ethics. When you interview someone for the podcast, protect their privacy and dignity. Get their consent before you record, and remember that recordings may be used only for course assessment. They may not be shared without the interviewee's consent.
    AI declaration. If a task permits AI use, submit the Student AI Declaration Form with it (tool used, purpose, and a statement that you reviewed the output and take responsibility for it), and cite the tool in APA 7th style. AI is not permitted in the final exam.

    Data & Sources

    Where to find reliable numbers and primary documents. Each card suggests one thing to try with the source, which is a good way into the podcast's data-analysis step (due Week 8).

    Before you quote a number, check three things: who counted it (a government, UNHCR, IOM or an NGO), what it counts (stocks or flows, people or applications, registered or estimated) and when it was counted. Most disagreements between migration statistics come down to one of these three.
