The Politics of International Migration

Migration was long marginal to both policymaking and IR scholarship. Since the 1970s it has moved steadily up the agenda of states, especially developed states, and today it is becoming more political and more politicized every day. This seminar engages with the debates, theories and research on the politics of migratory movements and post-migratory processes. It is organized around three subsections: migrants, refugees and diasporas.

“International migration is an inherently political phenomenon, in that it entails not merely physical relocation but a change of jurisdiction from one state to another and eventually also a change of membership from one community to another.”— Aristide Zolberg

Course learning outcomes

Students who complete this course will be able to:

  • IEvaluate the political dimensions of international migration.
  • IIGain knowledge about migration and asylum movements in history and in the current period.
  • IIIMake sense of the relationship between social processes that cause migration and those that migration creates.
  • IVComprehend the legal and institutional processes of different types of migration.
  • VEvaluate migration processes in Türkiye in the context of its global position, together with their causes and consequences.

How this seminar works

Every week

Read before class

The reading pack is on MS Teams. Weekly readings are required for everyone and form the basis of that week's discussion paper. Case readings apply the debates to specific contexts and are the basis for reaction papers.

From Week 4

Students lead

Each week one student presents a discussion paper and moderates, and one or two students present reaction papers on the case readings.

All semester

60% of the grade. You develop a research proposal of about 4,000 words in stages, from research question to final paper. See the Research Proposal tab.
